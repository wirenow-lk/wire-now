White House withdraws nomination of Heidi Overton to lead FDA

Politics📍 United States 🇺🇸 · 2026-11-18 · 📰 Reported by 3 outlets

📌 What happened

The White House withdrew Dr. Heidi Overton’s nomination to lead the FDA a day before a Senate health committee vote. Overton, a physician and abortion opponent with strong Trump ties, faced bipartisan concerns over her stance on vaccines and scientific independence. Reproductive rights groups celebrated the withdrawal, warning about future anti‑abortion nominees.

📖 The full story

The White House dropped Overton’s nomination after Trump nominated her in August. Overton, a physician and prominent abortion opponent, has spent most of her career working for Trump or conservative groups. Her nomination was seen as a move to bring a stabilizing Trump loyalist to the agency after a tumultuous year under Trump’s first FDA commissioner, Marty Makary.

Overton faced bipartisan Senate questions about her willingness to break from Trump, especially his unsupported claims about childhood vaccine dangers. Republican Senator Bill Cassidy of Louisiana, who chairs the Senate health committee, remained undecided ahead of Wednesday’s committee vote. Cassidy had previously called Overton’s support for breaking up combination vaccines “almost disqualifying” and pressed her on scientific independence during a September hearing.

A reproductive rights group praised the withdrawal. Mini Timmaraju, president and CEO of Reproductive Freedom for All, said Overton posed a serious threat to medication abortion access and warned about future nominees. Overton’s 2023 policy brief urged strict regulation of the abortion pill mifeprestone and criticized mailing of abortion pills. To be confirmed, Overton would have needed all 12 Republican votes on the committee; Democrats hold 11 seats. Republicans are racing to confirm Trump’s remaining nominees before the Senate Democrats take power in January.
