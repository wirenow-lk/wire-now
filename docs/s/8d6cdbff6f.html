A clinic is offering to reassess ADHD and autism diagnoses - and hundreds of people are interested

Health📍 Sweden 🇸🇪 · 2026-11-28 · BBC World

📌 What happened

A government-funded clinic in Sweden called Mottagning Neuropsykiatri offers free reassessments for ADHD and autism diagnoses, believed to be the first of its kind worldwide. People like Freya and Johanna seek reassessment because their diagnoses prevent them from joining the police or other professions. The clinic focuses on current symptoms rather than past history.

📖 The full story

The clinic, Mottagning Neuropsykiatri, opened as ADHD and autism diagnosis rates rise globally. Some young adults, including Freya (not her real name) and Johanna, request reassessment because they no longer feel the diagnosis fits and it blocks career paths like policing. Freya was diagnosed at 12 but doubts it; Johanna was diagnosed at 14 and reassessed at 21 during a pilot, after which she no longer met criteria.

In Sweden, ADHD and autism historically barred people from military, police, train driver, and pilot roles. Since 2022, medication-free ADHD individuals can join the military; since 2021, those with ADHD or autism can apply to the police but must provide a medical certificate costing about 15,000 SEK (£1,100) privately. The clinic offers free reassessment, which can update medical records and ease job applications.

Medical consensus from WHO and UK bodies says autism is lifelong and ADHD persists into adulthood. Lead psychologist Sebastian Lundström says the clinic does not 'de-diagnose' but reassesses current functioning, adding a note to records rather than erasing the original diagnosis. He suggests some may have been misdiagnosed or a minority may outgrow symptoms. Critics like Navid Ghannad of Autism Skåne warn that removing diagnoses risks future support access if symptoms return, and could deepen stigma. Lundström says the risk is low, noting that during the pilot some who wanted to lose their diagnosis were told they still met criteria.

💡 Why it matters

The clinic highlights tension between medical consensus that ADHD and autism are lifelong and a system where diagnoses create employment barriers. Reassessments may help individuals access jobs but risk reinforcing stigma and leaving people without support if symptoms reappear.
