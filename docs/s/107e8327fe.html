📖 The full story

The Pew Research Center, in a report released on Tuesday and reported by Al Jazeera, projects that Islam will become the world’s largest religion by 2035, overtaking Christianity. The study covers 201 countries and territories, representing 99.98 percent of the global population, and examines seven religious groups from 2020 to 2070. It is part of the Pew‑Templeton Global Religious Futures Project and warns that the findings are not intended to predict all possible future changes.

According to the report, Muslims have the highest fertility rate at 3.1 children per woman, which Pew says is one of the factors making them the world’s fastest‑growing religious group, while Christians have the second‑highest at 2.5, above the replacement level of about 2.1. Both groups are expected to keep pace with overall world population growth, with Muslims potentially gaining 1.6 billion people through natural increase compared to 792 million for Christians. The study also projects a net loss of 8 million Muslims and 105 million Christians due to people joining or leaving the faiths, and notes that the number of Muslims converting to Christianity is only slightly greater than the reverse.

The report finds that most of the projected growth in the Muslim population will occur in countries where Muslims already form a majority, including 53 countries that are expected to gain an additional 1.2 billion Muslims by 2070, representing 74 percent of the total growth. By 2070, Muslims are projected to make up 95 percent of the population in the Middle East‑North Africa region and to surpass the religiously unaffiliated in the Asia‑Pacific region, while Pakistan’s Muslim population is expected to double from 227 million in 2020 to 461 million in 2070, overtaking Indonesia. Christian growth will be concentrated in 120 countries where Christians are already the majority, with an overall increase of 630 million, accounting for 92 percent of global Christian growth.
