📖 The full story

Mexican authorities have arrested Fausto Isidro "Chapo Isidro" Meza Flores, a drug lord on the FBI's 10 most wanted list, after a shoot-out. Mexico's defence secretariat said special forces detained him and seven other people in Guasave, in the state of Sinaloa, on Friday. Seven suspects were killed in the operation, and so were four members of the Mexican army who were travelling to reinforce security in Guasave.

The secretariat said the forces used US intelligence on his whereabouts, moved in with what it called "stealth and surprise" and set a perimeter to stop him escaping and reinforcements arriving, but came under attack from gunfire and explosives before entering the property and arresting him. Videos circulating online appear to show helicopters firing streams of bullets towards the ground in Guasave, and people at a baseball stadium fleeing as they hear shooting sounds. Eight people were arrested in total, including his second-in-command, who is also his cousin, and his brother-in-law, the group's financial operator; a grenade launcher, other weapons, drugs and cash were seized, and while Meza Flores, his cousin and his brother-in-law were flown to Mexico City to face prosecution, the other five remained in Sinaloa.

Meza Flores, who is 44, leads the Meza Flores Organisation, also known as the Guasave Cartel, and is thought to have been active in drug trafficking since the 1990s, evading authorities until now by keeping a relatively low profile. He was wanted in Mexico on organised crime charges, and was first charged with drug trafficking in the US in 2012 and charged again in 2019, accused of conspiring to manufacture and distribute large quantities of cocaine, heroin, methamphetamine and marijuana between 2005 and 2019. He was originally a high-ranking member of the Beltrán Leyva Organization, but a split between rival factions led to a surge of violence and prompted him to set up his own cartel by 2013, and his group has since fought what US officials called an "extremely violent turf war" with the Sinaloa Cartel, one of the most powerful trafficking gangs, which operates in the same region.

The FBI accuses him of "flooding the US with fentanyl, cocaine, heroin and other deadly drugs", and the US is seeking his extradition. He was added to the bureau's most-wanted list in 2025, accused of "heinous crimes" including kidnapping, extortion and torture, after Mexican authorities reportedly linked his cartel to 20 million fentanyl pills seized in 2024; then-acting FBI director Brian Driscoll said Meza Flores and his organisation had unleashed a "scourge of illicit drugs" into the US via its southern border with Mexico. Mexico has stepped up efforts to tackle drug-related crime after coming under pressure from US President Donald Trump, who made stemming the flow of drugs across the southern border a key election pledge, while Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum has taken a tougher stance on cartels to resist US intervention on Mexican soil, welcoming closer collaboration but resisting US boots on the ground. The US Consulate General in the city of Hermosillo said it was "monitoring reports of ongoing security operations, armed clashes, road blockades, and vehicle fires that occurred overnight in Ahome, Guamúchil, Guasave, and Navolato" after the arrest, and advised US citizens to stay alert for potential violence and residents to remain sheltered in place.
