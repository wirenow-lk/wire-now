Appeal Court stresses need to protect elephants as national heritage

Society📍 Sri Lanka 🇱🇰 · 2026-11-20 · The Island

📌 What happened

The Court of Appeal emphasised that elephants must be safeguarded as a vital national resource and heritage. The bench made this observation while hearing a legal challenge against a government policy permitting firearms for farmers. The court noted that international tourists visit Sri Lanka to see wildlife rather than large buildings.

📖 The full story

The Sri Lanka Wildlife and Nature Protection Society filed a petition asking the court to overturn the government's decision to issue guns to farmers for crop protection. The case was heard on Tuesday by Court of Appeal President Justice Rohitha Abeysuriya and Justice Priyantha Fernando. The petitioner's lawyer, President's Counsel Sanjeewa Jayawardena, warned that giving firearms to farmers seriously imperils wild animals, especially elephants, noting that the tusker Kawanthissa had already been shot dead.

Jayawardena informed the judges that the minimum landholding required to obtain a firearm had recently been reduced by the government from five acres to two acres, which could lead to many more weapons being handed out. He revealed that 305 firearms were distributed by 2002, 490 by 2026, and data indicated a total of 3,207 firearms have been issued so far.

Justice Abeysuriya stated that the court places significant weight on protecting the environment and wildlife, stressing that elephants are a national asset and that tourists do not travel to the island for tall buildings. The court said it would seek proposals from all parties on ways to keep elephants safe. Named respondents include Deputy Defence Minister retired Major General Aruna Jayasekara, Defence Ministry Secretary Air Vice Marshal Sampath Thuyacontha, and Environment Minister Dr. Dammika Patabendi.

💡 Why it matters

The case highlights concerns that loosening rules on issuing firearms to farmers puts endangered wildlife, particularly the national elephant population, at severe risk.
