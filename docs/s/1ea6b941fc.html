Budget 2027: Rising wave of protests

Politics📍 Colombo, Sri Lanka 🇱🇰 · 2026-11-26 · The Island

📌 What happened

Parliament is debating Budget 2027 while trade unions and state workers protest against it. The Opposition, including former Minister Prof. G. L. Peiris, says the budget fails to address unemployment, living costs, and welfare. The government defends its budget and claims it rebuilt the economy, but critics warn of slower growth and higher inflation.

📖 The full story

Government doctors have threatened trade union action over their grievances, and nurses, public sector engineers, teachers, principals, university academics, non-academic workers, and Development Officers are also protesting. The government won elections by promising to grant pay hikes twice a year, and the JVP-led NPP secured nearly four-fifths of the postal votes at the 2024 parliamentary election, compared with 61.56% of the overall vote. Government leaders have boasted of overflowing coffers.

Former Minister Prof. G. L. Peiris is among those leading the Opposition's campaign against Budget 2027. Speaking at the People's United Opposition political office on Flower Road, Colombo, on Tuesday, he said the budget lacks a practical programme to address unemployment, rising living costs, and declining welfare. He noted that unemployment among educated youth stood at 43%, with approximately 40,000 graduates unable to find jobs, and that nearly half of those unable to secure employment were compelled to seek jobs overseas, while the departure of doctors created approximately 7,000 vacancies in the medical sector and universities have only 6,000 teachers against a requirement of 12,000.

The Opposition says the government has failed to utilise the funds allocated for development, citing a World Bank report that only 16.4% of capital expenditure allocated in last year's Budget had been utilised by September this year, with pending use by year-end expected to reach only 30% to 40% of the allocation. It argues that employment generation requires capital investment to revitalise the economy, attract foreign direct investment, and generate jobs. Ruling party members, however, describe the budget as a magic wand and say the public must exercise patience.

Prof. Peiris compared economic growth of 5% in 2024 with a slowdown from 5.1% in the second quarter of 2025 to 4.2% in the corresponding period this year. He said inflation had risen from 0.8% to 8.3%, while the dollar had climbed from Rs. 293 to Rs. 337. He added that foreign reserves had risen by US$6 billion under Wickremesinghe, compared with about US$1 billion under the present government, and Cabinet Spokesman Dr Nalinda Jayatissa said Wickremesinghe made 33 foreign trips costing Rs. 1,010 million while President Anura Kumara Dissanayake spent Rs. 31.7 million on eight foreign trips, with 421 individuals accompanying Wickremesinghe from his visit to the United Kingdom on September 17, 2022, to his tour of India on June 8, 2024.
