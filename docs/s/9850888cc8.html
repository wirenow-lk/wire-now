Cyclonic Storm Akvan: Warning issued to Naval and fishing communities

Weather📍 Kankasanthurai to Pottuvil via Trincomalee and Batticaloa, Sri Lanka 🇱🇰 · 2026-11-17 · 📰 Reported by 3 outlets

📌 What happened

The Department of Meteorology warned naval and fishing communities not to enter several sea areas because of bad weather from Cyclonic Storm Akvan. The storm strengthened and is expected to get stronger.

📖 The full story

The Newswire reported that the Department of Meteorology in Sri Lanka gave a warning to naval and fishing communities. It said they should not go into some sea areas because of bad weather from Cyclonic Storm Akvan. The advice was to stay away from the listed sea zones.

The Met Department said the groups should not enter deep sea areas between 5°N and 18°N and 80°E and 95°E. It also named sea areas from Kankasanthurai to Pottuvil via Trincomalee and Batticaloa. People already working there were told to go to safer places.

The warning came after a deep depression over the Southeast Bay of Bengal and adjoining Southwest Bay of Bengal became Cyclonic Storm Akvan. The storm was about 640 kilometres from Trincomalee at around 11.30 p.m. yesterday (16). It is expected to become a Severe Cyclonic Storm over the Westcentral Bay of Bengal by 5.30 p.m. today (17), before strengthening further into a Very Severe Cyclonic Storm by November 19, and it may cross the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts in India between Nellore and Tuni around 11.30 a.m. on November 20.

The department said showers or thunder may happen at several sea places around Sri Lanka today. It expected north-westerly or westerly winds of 30–40 kmph, with speeds increasing up to 60 kmph at times in affected sea areas, and wind speeds may reach 50 kmph in other sea areas around the island. It also warned that eastern and northeastern coastal stretches could become rough at times, other sea areas may be fairly rough, and strong gusty winds of 60–70 kmph and very rough seas were possible during thunder, while fishing and naval groups were urged to stay vigilant and follow future forecasts and advisories.
