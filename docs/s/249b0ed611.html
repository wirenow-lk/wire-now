'Poison seller' Kenneth Law apologises in court to the families of his victims

Kenneth Law, a 61-year-old Canadian former chef who sold toxic chemicals through online suicide forums in 40 countries, apologised in court on Friday to the families of his victims. 📖 The full story

Kenneth Law, a 61-year-old former chef from Canada, sold toxic chemicals to people he met in online suicide forums, sending about 1,200 packages to recipients in 40 countries, with roughly a quarter of them going to the UK. On Friday he spoke at his sentencing hearing in an Ontario court, telling the families of victims that he deeply regrets his actions and will never repeat them. He said words can never replace the loss of their loved ones, offered his deepest condolences, and said he remains remorseful and sorry.

Law pleaded guilty in May to 14 counts of aiding the suicide deaths of 14 people in Ontario, Canada. He has also admitted causing the deaths of dozens of victims in England and Wales. Under a plea deal, Canadian authorities withdrew more serious charges of murder, and he will not face charges in the UK or over any British deaths.

At Friday's hearing, prosecutors asked the judge for a 25-year prison term and stressed that many victims were young and struggling with mental distress. Because he would get credit for time served since his May 2023 arrest, the recommended sentence would mean just under 20 years in prison. Crown Attorney Kathleen Doherty called Law a predator who took advantage of vulnerable people, saying they needed help but found Law and his products for death, and she said his motive was financial: if people could pay, he sold to them, with no questions about their circumstances.

Defence lawyer Matthew Gourlay disagreed, arguing Law was not responsible for the victims' battles with mental illness, that each incident is its own unique tragedy, and that while Law may be a common denominator, he was not the cause of the hardships that made people seek his products; Gourlay said Law held a misguided belief that he was helping, and asked for a 12-year sentence. The hearing followed an emotional few days of testimony in September, when grieving family members and friends told the court about the pain Law caused. Those impact statements will be taken into account at sentencing, which is due at a later date, and the BBC pointed readers to its Action Line for organisations that offer information to anyone affected.
