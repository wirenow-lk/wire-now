HRCSL orders probe into damage allegedly caused to Sigiriya Rock in 2017

Society📍 Sigiriya, Sri Lanka 🇱🇰 · 2026-11-24 · The Island

📌 What happened

The Sri Lanka Human Rights Commission ordered an investigation into damage allegedly caused to Sigiriya Rock during 2017 conservation work, after a petition by Deeman Ananda. The Commission found drilling, cable installation, and use of modern materials had damaged the rock, and directed several government bodies to take prompt action.

📖 The full story

The Sri Lanka Human Rights Commission issued a determination on October 20 based on a petition filed by Deeman Ananda. The petition alleged that development and conservation work carried out at the Sigiriya World Heritage site in 2017 had destroyed archaeological evidence, including drilling holes, attaching machinery, altering the Lion’s Paw, and removing ancient inscriptions.

An on-site inspection found a cable system installed from the base to the summit to transport materials, with equipment attached to the rock. The rock had been drilled using electrical equipment, and modern mortar and cement were used in conserving ancient mortar. The Commission said the respondents — the Department of Archaeology, Central Cultural Fund, Ministry of Cultural Affairs, and the Sigiriya Project of the Central Cultural Fund — failed to prove the work had proper approval or followed formal procedure.

The Commission concluded the work caused damage to the rock, the most significant natural and cultural feature of Sigiriya, and warned that development without proper procedures could violate cultural and environmental heritage rights. However, the allegation that ancient inscriptions were removed could not be substantiated; the Department of Archaeology said only seven inscriptions had been identified in the area. The Commission stressed the need for a proper mechanism under joint supervision of the Department of Archaeology, the Cultural Triangle, and the National Building Research Organisation for activities of national importance, and made several recommendations to protect archaeological sites and preserve their historical value.

💡 Why it matters

The Commission warned that development without proper procedures could violate rights relating to cultural and environmental heritage.
