📖 The full story

Amnesty International renewed its calls for accountability and an end to decades-long impunity for conflict-related crimes in Sri Lanka. This came after a new report by the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) looking at attacks on humanitarian assistance during the internal armed conflict.

Amnesty's South Asia Director, Smriti Singh, said no progress had been made on crimes under international law, and that truth, justice and reparations remain elusive for hundreds of thousands of victims. She said the report showed successive Sri Lankan governments were unwilling to investigate allegations and ensure accountability for abuses committed during the conflict that ended in 2009.

Singh said impunity continues even as mass graves are still being identified, including in Chemmani, where recent exhumations uncovered the remains of more than 583 individuals, including children. She said attacks on humanitarian activities in Sri Lanka had become a model and precursor to similar incidents in conflicts around the world, and warned that impunity there could encourage crimes and undermine international law elsewhere.

Amnesty stressed the need for continued international scrutiny and urged UN member states to use the OHCHR Sri Lanka Accountability Project's resources, including through universal jurisdiction. The report, titled 'I Died Many Times: Attacks on Humanitarian Assistance in Sri Lanka, 20 Years On', examines attacks on humanitarian personnel and operations and the restriction and denial of aid by all parties in the conflict's final phase. The Accountability Project was set up under UN Human Rights Council Resolution 46/1 of 2021 to collect, consolidate, analyze and preserve evidence of serious violations, abuses and related crimes.
