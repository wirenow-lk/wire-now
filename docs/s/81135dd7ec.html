New court order: Why was BYD banned from selling its new Ti7 SUV in Europe??

Business📍 The Hague, Netherlands, Netherlands 🇳🇱 · 2026-11-21 · Newswire

📌 What happened

A Dutch court issued a preliminary injunction on November 20. It blocked BYD from selling its Fang Cheng Bao Ti7 SUV in the European Union. The court found the Ti7 had a similar overall visual impression to the Land Rover Defender and that this infringed design rights.

📖 The full story

The source is a newswire. BYD, a Chinese electric vehicle giant, got a legal setback. A Dutch court in The Hague issued a preliminary injunction on November 20 after a design rights dispute with British luxury vehicle maker Jaguar Land Rover (JLR).

The court found the BYD Ti7 created a similar overall visual impression to the Land Rover Defender. It cited similarities in overall proportions, rounded shoulders, wheel arches, and window lines. The injunction stopped BYD from importing, manufacturing, offering for sale, selling, storing, or delivering the Ti7 in the European Union.

The restriction applied only to the Ti7 and did not affect BYD’s other vehicles. The Fang Cheng Bao Ti7 is a seven-seat plug-in hybrid SUV from BYD’s Fang Cheng Bao sub-brand. BYD maintained the vehicle was independently designed and did not infringe JLR’s intellectual property rights.

The decision was a legal victory for JLR, which sought to protect the Defender SUV design. But the ruling was a preliminary injunction, not a final judgment after a full trial. The court noted BYD had no concrete plans to bring the Ti7 into the European market at that time, and the case highlights increasing competition as Chinese brands expand.

💡 Why it matters

The case highlights increasing competition between Chinese automakers and established European manufacturers as Chinese brands continue expanding their international presence.
